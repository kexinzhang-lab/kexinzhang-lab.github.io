research

Computational imaging and discovery, from molecules to cells.

From noisy images to molecular discovery

We combine cryo-electron microscopy and tomography (cryo-EM/ET), AI, and structural biology to find molecules and understand their structures and states. By bringing these approaches together, we aim to uncover hidden patterns in how molecules are organized within cells.

01 DiffPose

Faster ways to read cryo-EM images

Which way is each molecule facing? We use AI and imaging physics to match a molecule's orientation to its microscope image. Faster alignment helps us turn large image collections into 3D structures.

Explore this project →

02 GisAPR

Building atomic models from images

We adjust the positions of atoms to match microscope images directly. This connects structural models to the original measurements, without first building a 3D map.

Explore this project →

03 2D template matching

Seeing smaller proteins more clearly

Small proteins are difficult to see in noisy images. We use known structures as guides to find and align them, revealing finer details of their shapes and the pockets where drugs can bind.

Explore this project → eLife, 2026 ↗

04 2DTM p-value

Finding weak signals in noisy images

Is a faint feature a real molecule or just noise? We developed a statistical score that helps tell them apart, making it easier to detect challenging targets while controlling false positives.

Explore this project → IUCrJ, 2025 ↗

05 In situ classification

Locating molecular machines inside cells

We identify different stages of ribosome assembly directly in images of cells. Mapping where these molecular machines are helps us understand how cells build them, without taking them out of their native environment.

Explore this project → eLife, 2022 ↗
